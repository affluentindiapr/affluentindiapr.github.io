Are Awards a Scam? The Question You Should Be Asking Instead

Not every award works the same way. Here's how to tell the difference.

By Bhavika Valecha, Founder, Affluent India PR · PR and reputation content creator and educator

Every time someone shares an award win online, the comments say the same thing: awards are a scam, and you can just pay for one.

At Affluent India PR, we've managed award nominations and wins for our clients over the years. And from that experience, I think we're asking the wrong question.

The real question isn't "did they win?"

The question isn't whether someone won an award. It's what the process behind that award was.

Not all awards work the same way

Awards vary a lot in how they're decided:

  • Some are judged by independent juries.
  • Some are editorially driven, chosen by a publication's own team.
  • Some require a nomination fee.
  • Others require a very detailed application.

Yes, some awards have weak credibility

Are there awards with weak credibility? Absolutely. But that doesn't mean every award is meaningless.

The internet tends to put every award in the same bucket and call all of them fake. That isn't fair to the people who earned theirs.

The trophy isn't the valuable part

I've seen entrepreneurs and creators create real impact, and sometimes an award is simply recognition of that work. The trophy itself isn't the valuable part. The work behind it is.

What to ask next time

So the next time you see someone win an award, don't immediately ask how much they paid. Ask what they did to deserve it.
